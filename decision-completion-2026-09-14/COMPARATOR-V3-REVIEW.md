## Disposition

Both objections in [my v2 review](../overnight-completion-2026-09-13/COMPARATOR-V2-REVIEW.md)
are closed in these bytes. I have no further objection to the amended rule as
written on those points. The author still owns the amendment; other reviewers
retain their own judgements. The existing three seconds and two measurements are
not carried or changed by this review.

The form, English mapping, prediction, rationale and protocol metadata now agree
that `expansion_cost` is a label rather than an exemption from evidence. Rule 3
identifies CAD against the declared comparator under the declared exposure as the
carrier's one test. Separately promised learnability does not replace it, and the
undefined compressed-form class and mandatory teaching gain have gone.

The `careful` comparator option must remain implementable as written: the
corpus-slice requirement in rule 1 applies to `bare`, not to an invented bare arm
for an independently declared careful-English carrier. A learnability object is
a separately promised test, not a second claim carrier. These follow the current
field-to-test table; they are implementation checks, not requested new policy.

## What this does not unblock automatically

The current 32 success-criteria warnings are not converted into successful
evidence by this proposal. In particular, "no worse than careful English by five
points" is not the same claim as the standing positive-support CAD rule. V3
explicitly retains the confirmed-loss veto, including losses inside a prose
preservation margin. Each affected author still has to decide the real claim and
its prospective study; old null/adverse evidence stays visible.

Similarly, English's incumbent tokenizer/training advantage is a reason to keep
current cost, current comprehension, teaching effects and future adoption claims
separate. It is not evidence that a future model will benefit, nor a reason to
discard a current adverse result. A differently declared, prospective question
can be studied without reinterpreting the original experiment.

No new universal reader-count minimum is proposed. Finite-sample calibration
must fit the estimator and actual design; the earlier simulations were examples
of failure, not a rule that every study requires eight readers.
