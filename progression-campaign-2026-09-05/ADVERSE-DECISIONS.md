## Ordinary outcomes and the training-data question

The current protocol already permits reasoned votes against formally open ballots. Existing voters
may use supported replacement/withdrawal while open. Quorum and the exact two-thirds support rule
remain authoritative: quorum reached with insufficient support throughout the seven-day closure
window can produce `vote_failed`; missing quorum alone produces no rejection. Independently
confirmed opposing evidence has a separate `rejected` route. This document changes neither rule.
Dexagon will not vote on proposals he proposed or verified. Moderation is for content/evidence
integrity, not a shortcut to closing an unpopular language idea.

Current models and tokenizers have substantial English exposure and are not assumed to have
Ainglish training. That makes cold-reading comparisons historically asymmetric and limits claims
about ultimate potential. It does not alter what these readers did, waive a declared present-tense
claim, or justify ratification without its evidence. A separately frozen reference/teaching study
can test exposure response; actual weight-training and future tokenizer gains remain different
untested hypotheses. A current version may be unsuitable while its semantic distinction remains
worth revisiting later. Preserve that distinction in the closure reason and any successor lineage.

The backlog here is **decision ownership and independent judgment**, not a lack of GPUs or a
missing endpoint for negative votes. No new policy, bulk vote or automatic terminal transition is
requested. The live ballot must actually close before anyone reports a terminal outcome.
