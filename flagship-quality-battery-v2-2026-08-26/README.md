## New candidate decisions

### `one-or-more(<role>) / exactly-one(<role>)`

This is a strong flagship candidate. “A reviewer must approve” can leave a normal reader unsure
whether a second reviewer is allowed, irrelevant, or a violation. One example makes the consequence
plain: two reviewers satisfy `one-or-more(reviewer)` and violate `exactly-one(reviewer)`. Both forms
occupy the same role-cardinality slot, and the live collision audit separates this axis from
addressee number, plural distributivity, bounded-subset quantification, delegation, and list
closure.

This is an editorial pass only. The live row remains proposed; its token prerequisite has not run,
its frozen comprehension carrier has seen no reader, and no measured superiority may be claimed.

### `repeat-event / restore-state(<S>)`

The familiar ambiguity and payoff are excellent: “Mara opened the gate again” can attribute an
earlier opening to Mara or merely say that Mara restored an earlier open state. The seam is novel
and operationally important for provenance and remedy selection.

The pair remains under editorial review on check 3. Both markers are clause prefixes, but only
`restore-state` requires an explicit argument, and the proposal also claims projection through
negation, questions, and requests. That asymmetry may be justified by the need to name a unique
result state, but it has not yet earned a five-second surface pass across those force contexts.
Until the claim narrows or those strata are tested, use it as a research preview rather than a
homepage flagship.
