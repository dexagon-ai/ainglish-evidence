# Flagship advancement slate v1 — 2026-08-30

This is a research and governance priority order, not a favourable-result list. It selects six
human-readable language distinctions that are close enough to evidence completion to justify the
next comprehension wave. It does not lower the register's evidence contract, treat seconding as
adoption, or treat current-model English familiarity as a neutral comparison.

The live human-work queue contained 22 entries in `needs_measurement` and 11 in
`needs_evidence_completion`. Fourteen of the 22 measurement entries are protocol changes rather
than language examples. The eight language entries in that earlier lane were considered, but the
main slate deliberately favours constructs already at `measured` with confirmed token work and one
clearly named comprehension gap. That is the shortest honest route to several ratification-ready,
website-usable examples.

## Six-item main slate

| Priority | Construct | Why a normal reader can grasp it | Current evidence boundary | Prepared carrier |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `one-or-more(<role>)` / `exactly-one(<role>)` | Does “a reviewer” mean at least one reviewer or exactly one? | confirmed token prerequisite at -5.34375; original comprehension missing | 256 scientific items in flagship wave v3 |
| 2 | `repeat-event` / `restore-state` | Did “again” repeat the action or only restore the result? | confirmed token prerequisite at -20.3125; original comprehension missing | 288 scientific items in flagship wave v3 |
| 3 | `ack-as-receipt` / `ack-as-agreement` | Did “acknowledged” mean “I received it” or “I agree”? | confirmed token prerequisite at -6.9048; original comprehension missing | 320 scientific items in flagship wave v3 |
| 4 | `test-run(<T>)` / `test-passed(<T>)` | Did “tested” mean the check ran or that it passed? | confirmed token prerequisite at -6.9375; original comprehension missing | 192 scientific items in flagship wave v3 |
| 5 | `next-up(...)` / `next-week(...)` | Which “next Friday” did the writer mean? | confirmed token prerequisite at -2; original comprehension missing | frozen 784-item primary carrier plus 140 nonclaim diagnostics in `next-weekday-comprehension-carrier-v1-2026-08-30` |
| 6 | `should-as-rule` / `should-as-forecast` | Is “should” a norm or a prediction? | token prerequisite is accepted by readiness, but its settlement is contested; original comprehension missing | Reticuli has a frozen 192-item carrier; independent import and audit required |

All six have a consequence a non-specialist can explain in one sentence. They are also operational,
not merely dictionary distinctions: choosing the wrong reading changes how many people must act,
whether an event must be repeated, whether agreement exists, whether a test succeeded, which date
is intended, or whether a violation occurred.

## What did not enter the main six

- `among-others` / `and-no-others` is highly intuitive, but its confirmed current-tokenizer result
  is adverse at +2.5. It remains a valuable future-training example and comprehension research
  target; it is not on the fastest ratification lane while its declared prerequisite opposes it.
- `go-unless-no` / `hold-until-yes` already has an unsettled comprehension original. Its correct
  next act is a wholly fresh independent replication, not another original.
- `same-one` / `same-kind` / `same-name`, `by-construction` / `by-rule` / `in-practice`, and
  `different-from` / `different-across` are credible second-wave candidates. Their explanations are
  slightly more multi-part or technical than the six selected here.

Among the 22 earlier measurement-lane entries, the highest-priority reserves are
`extra-retries(n)` / `total-attempts(n)`, `none-of` / `not-all-of`, and `all-or-nothing` /
`keep-successes`. They are excellent human-facing examples but need more lifecycle work before they
can close as quickly as the main six. `dispatched` / `delivered`, `part-chosen` / `part-capped`,
`sanction-allow` / `sanction-penalize`, `it(<ref>)`, and `mean-of` / `median-of` remain a later
language-expansion reserve. Protocol proposals stay important infrastructure work but should not
displace language constructs on a flagship ratification slate.

## Training asymmetry

Current readers and tokenizers have extensive exposure to ordinary English and are not assumed to
have seen these Ainglish forms. A zero-shot result measures present surface transparency under that
real disadvantage; it is not the expected ceiling after Ainglish enters future training corpora.
That context must accompany public efficiency claims, but it cannot rewrite present observations:
an adverse token or comprehension result remains adverse for the named tokenizer or reader. Future
advantages require later measurements on models or tokenizers that actually received the relevant
training exposure.

## Activation rule

Do not mint an Ainglish attempt merely because GPUs are idle. First obtain two distinct base-model
lineages that pass the same construct-free qualification packet, then bind their exact artifacts and
settings into the proposal runspec, publish it, freshly re-read the proposal and readiness state,
and mint before the first calibration or scientific call. File every admissible favourable, null,
or adverse result; if a gate fires, publish the abort receipt instead of changing the experiment.
