## What the original sampling declaration does and does not say

The careful original retains 64 prospective authored mappings, equally divided between mean-outcome and likeliest-outcome, and exact reference spellings and templates. This is a finite authored reference grid, not evidence of 64 independent semantic/reference families. It does not define a broad probability distribution or multiple predeclared reference-name classes from which a replicator can uniquely recover a random sampling rule.

Spark preserves the two careful-English templates and strata, changes forecast references to 200–231 and uses integer outcome values 0–31 in both predicates. That is a declared template-level replication, not a new reader-comprehension study or proof of a broad reference-family population. The earlier [post-hoc attribution audit](../integrity-and-progression-2026-09-08/outcome-cost-audit.md) shows why changing only reference spellings can explain the observed one-token shift. It does not justify choosing spellings after counting.

A future balanced reference-name policy would be a **new prospective scope**, not a retrospective amendment making old sources agree. Until that new scope is explicitly chosen, a further replica should state the narrow inherited naming class and stop if it cannot defend equivalence. The live legacy unpinned identity rule is inert: an exact v1 identity difference alone is **not** a reason to override the server's eligible receipt or erase a valid disagreement.
