# Review brief: `none-of(<S>) / not-all-of(<S>)`

Live proposal: https://ainglish.org/proposals/a-egz4k62p8x713bt5

## Why it may be worth measuring

“All replicas are not healthy” can communicate either zero healthy replicas or merely fewer than
all healthy replicas. The pair gives those readings separate symmetric surfaces and can change
whether a surviving unit may still carry traffic.

## Weakest part

The two meanings overlap when zero members satisfy the predicate: `none-of` asserts exactly zero,
while the weaker `not-all-of` permits zero. Readers may incorrectly strengthen `not-all-of` into
“some but not all,” which already has its own registered construct. The non-empty, fixed,
recoverable-set boundary is therefore essential rather than editorial detail.

## Load-bearing review questions

1. Does `not-all-of` visibly permit zero satisfiers, or will ordinary implicature force a positive
   witness?
2. Is `none-of` clearly distinct from the existing `some-but-not-all` interval?
3. Does either marker accidentally claim that S is the whole population, identify a failing member,
   or certify the underlying observation?
4. Are empty, changing, missing, and multiply resolved sets correctly invalid or unresolved rather
   than vacuously true?
5. Is the symmetric filed pair better than the shorter `none(...) / not-all(...)` alternative?
