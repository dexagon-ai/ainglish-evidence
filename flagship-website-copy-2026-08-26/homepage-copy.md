# Ainglish makes hidden choices explicit

Ordinary English often makes readers guess. Ainglish adds small, readable distinctions where that
guess changes who acts, what is known, or what is allowed.

## Four ratified examples

### Does “we” include you?

**Ordinary English:** We will review the draft.

**Ainglish:** `we-including-you will review the draft.` / `we-excluding-you will review the draft.`

`we-including-you` includes the reader; `we-excluding-you` does not.

Ratified in Ainglish 0.10.0.

### Does “you” mean one person or the whole group?

**Ordinary English:** You should approve the change.

**Ainglish:** `you-one should approve the change.` / `you-all should approve the change.`

`you-one` addresses one recipient; `you-all` addresses the whole group.

Ratified in Ainglish 0.30.0.

### Is the fact unknown, or is the decision unfinished?

**Ordinary English:** The region is undecided.

**Ainglish:** `fact-not-known — region.` / `choice-not-made — region.`

`fact-not-known` says the evidence is missing; `choice-not-made` says the decision is still
pending.

Ratified in Ainglish 0.6.0.

### May the task be handed off?

**Ordinary English:** Complete the audit yourself.

**Ainglish:** `Complete the audit, no-delegation.` / `Complete the audit,
one-hop-delegation-allowed.`

`no-delegation` forbids handoff. `one-hop-delegation-allowed` permits a direct delegate but no
further handoff.

Ratified in Ainglish 0.8.0.

## More ratified distinctions

- `each-alone / as-one`: every member acts separately, or the group acts collectively. The entry is
  ratified; its registered comprehension evidence remains disputed, so do not advertise a settled
  measured benefit.
- `or-both / not-both`: both alternatives are allowed, or both together are forbidden. The entry is
  ratified; do not imply token efficiency or an overall measured win.
- `start-by / complete-by`: the action must begin by the deadline, or it must be successfully
  completed by the deadline. The entry is ratified; do not claim a measured benefit.
- `true-as-worded / false-as-worded`: answer the sentence exactly as written, including its
  negation. The entry is ratified; its overall experimental record is not uniformly supportive.

## In the measurement pipeline

These are candidate examples, not published Ainglish entries:

- `moved-earlier / moved-later` states which direction a rescheduled event moved. Stage: measured,
  not ratified; comprehension and tag-fidelity work remain incomplete.
- `among-others / and-no-others` says whether a list may omit further members or is complete. Stage:

- `one-or-more(<role>) / exactly-one(<role>)` distinguishes at least one role-holder from one and
  only one. Stage: proposed; independent review and both evidence metrics remain outstanding.

## What “ratified” means here

Ratified means that an entry passed the Ainglish register's governance process and was accepted at
the version shown. It does not mean that every human or model will understand the form, that the
form guarantees compliant behaviour, or that every supporting claim has been confirmed by a broad
human study.
