## What each control actually asks

| Dimension | Explicit fact that the control checks |
| --- | --- |
| Gender | Whether every referent is a woman; a non-woman is explicit in the negative case. No inference from a name or pronoun. |
| Known identity | Whether the recorder knows every referent's identity, separately from whether the text identifies someone to the reader. |
| Unanimity | Whether every member supported the proposal; passing is not necessarily unanimity. |
| All-members participation | Whether every member personally performed a task step; completion alone does not establish that. |
| Collective action | Whether the activity was one coordinated group event; membership and completion do not establish that. |

For each form/dimension, the packet has an explicit true fact, an explicit false
fact, and an undetermined record. Committee, team and ensemble controls preserve
the distinction between one referent entity and its several internal members.
The five questions require separate answers; success on one is not success on
another. The facts, golds and question wording are the review target.

The form slots are metadata: **the proposed number markers do not appear in
these prompts**. These are shared-context control concepts to be reviewed before
any target-bank creation. Later matched control renderings must keep facts
identical across marked/careful arms and receive their own frozen prompts and
independent execution records. The prototypes are not silently promoted to a
confirmatory sample or reused as previously unseen target items.

## Shortcut and observation checks

Each of the 30 semantic templates has three option orders: **90 prompt variants
are not 90 independent worlds**. The option permutations test positional
shortcuts only and cannot increase the statistical denominator.

The six synthetic shortcut witnesses yield, at every form/dimension endpoint:

- Always unknown: 0% on explicit-fact controls, despite 100% on undetermined ones.
- Always Yes or always No: 50% on explicit-fact controls.
- Always option position 0, 1 or 2: one third on both roles.

Thus none satisfies the accepted 90% positive-control floor. This is arithmetic
on synthetic answer functions, not observed model behaviour or a confidence bound.
Undetermined success is reported separately and cannot hide failed positive
controls. Missing observations make the endpoint incomplete; nulls are absence,
not silently counted as wrong or omitted from the plan. Malformed, duplicate or
cross-endpoint records are refused.

`score()` requires a distinct observation ID for every planned probe and checks
its dimension/form binding. That is a consistency check, **not authentication**:
renaming copied responses can only be detected against the eventual raw,
request-bound SDK/provider journals. The final execution plan must retain those
per-probe traces. No prototype ID scheme can prove an actual model call occurred.

These are also **not SDK planted-effect calibration rows**. Both arms must share
the same explicit facts, so these controls cannot replace the separate official
calibration that checks a reader's sensitivity to a planted difference.

For scale only, under an actually valid independent Bernoulli model, n successes
out of n give a one-sided 95% lower bound of `0.05 ** (1/n)`: at least 29 all-correct
independent observations are needed to put it above 0.90. Zero failures give upper
bound `1 - 0.05 ** (1/n)`: at least 59 are needed to put that below 0.05. These follow
by inverting the all-success/no-failure probability, not a fitted power analysis.
They do **not** justify these prototype templates, independence, future sample
size, a shared-frame model or a CAD difference interval. Actual errors require
more information, and repeated option orders supply none of that independence.
