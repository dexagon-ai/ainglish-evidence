## A potentially useful question, with a strong comparator

Do the markers help a reader bind **which particular outcome** was intended
when an ordinary incident handoff reports two outcomes close together? This
is plausible real usage: one requested change and one side effect, sometimes
foreseen, sometimes not. It is not a general intelligence puzzle, a deliberately
bad English baseline, or a demand for longer/more complex text in the English arm.

Use a shared, short plan/risk/discovery anchor and two completed action reports.
The question names one doer and one outcome. There are no ambiguous pronouns or
hidden domain facts. Both arms have the same anchor, incidents, order and facts.
Only the intention expression changes. Use the registered careful expansions
`deliberately` and `by mistake, without intending that outcome`; do not delete
their explicit intention information or pad the English. A negative-risk example
must not imply that knowing a risk means aiming at the outcome, or that lacking
intention removes responsibility.

Examples below are **expository review cases, excluded from any eventual test**:

1. Intended target, adverse-looking outcome: a conservator adopts an order to
   remove an old paper backing while preserving the label. The report says the
   backing was removed on-purpose/deliberately and the label detached
   by-accident/by mistake, without intending that outcome. Ask about removing
   the backing. Gold: yes. Harm/benefit is not the answer rule.
2. Unforeseen target: a stage technician adopts a plan to lower a backdrop and
   discovers only afterwards that a nearby prop label also detached. The report
   attributes the backdrop change on-purpose/deliberately and the label event
   by-accident/by mistake, without intending that outcome. Ask about detaching
   the label. Gold: no. Do not ask an unspecified unreported event instead.
3. Accepted-risk target: a sound editor's plan keeps the recorded introduction;
   a risk note says a splice might cut its first word, which is not part of the
   adopted objective. The report says a gap was shortened on-purpose/deliberately
   and the first word clipped by-accident/by mistake, without intending that
   outcome. Ask about clipping the word. Gold: no. Prior foresight remains true.
4. Positive but unwelcome target: a nursery worker adopts a quarantine instruction
   to discard one tray while preserving the adjacent tray. The specified tray
   was discarded on-purpose/deliberately; the adjacent tray was moved
   by-accident/by mistake, without intending that outcome. Ask about discarding
   the specified tray. Gold: yes. An unpleasant decision can still be intended.

The anchors still contain the semantic truth, as the author's prediction
requires. They therefore cannot establish a marker-alone effect. This design
tests contextual event binding, not whether readers can ignore all context.
Removing the anchors or replacing the question with a different task is **not**
silently allowed under this packet: the author must decide prospectively whether
that changes the declared claim and needs a successor. An exploratory original
on a different estimand would not automatically discharge this promise.

## Proposed final design, if accepted

- Two exact reader editions chosen **before** target outputs, on access and
  method grounds, never because one produced favourable errors. Both independent
  executors must be able to run the same complete roster/settings. Existing local
  editions are one possibility; a fixed remote roster is a new-instrument original,
  not an exact replication of Saturnia's local-edition study.
- 192 unique scientific incidents per study, in eight prospectively selected
  domains: 96 intended, 48 unforeseen unintended, 48 accepted-risk unintended;
  weights 2:1:1. Fresh events, not repeated worlds counted as independent items.
  All first/third-person and active/passive frames, target report position and
  option order balanced within reader and class. Actual SDK-runner checks must
  verify assignment by endpoint `name`, separately from roster identity.
- A wholly disjoint 192-incident replication bank, authored and frozen without
  seeing the first study's outputs. Review every gold against both arm texts.
  No defective English, answer-bearing filename/id, ambiguous gold, cross-arm
  factual change or repetition disguised as independent sample.
- The original's yes/no/cannot-tell intention question, with explicit target noun
  phrase when two outcomes coexist, subject to the author's scope acceptance.
  Keep the strict-positive rule, separate load-bearing strata and loss veto.
- SDK official interval/stratum methods, item-level replay and named-roster
  diagnostics; predeclare a domain sensitivity analysis as diagnostic, not a
  substitute statistical pass. Publish all outcomes, ceiling/floor findings,
  wrong/absent answers and failures. No enlarging the bank or replacing a reader
  after results. No claimed power estimate from the old confounded +4.17 point.
- Twelve new target-independent panel controls, both arms per reader: 384
  scientific +48 calibration = **432 calls per study**. Up to 96 qualification
  calls per study if both exact-roster receipts need renewal. Combined maximum
  across original and replication: **1,056 calls**, not booked or authorised now.
  Reader-derived controls cannot be selected for agreeing with this construct.
- Exact request body, response text, parse, gold and grade retained from the
  first call; redact transport credentials before journal writing. Preflight,
  then mint before target inference. The second run has its own fresh live
  eligibility, qualification and preregistration checks. An invalid/changed
  source or fired resource/transport gate stops the relevant replication route;
  it does not license an unregistered substitute or missing-result suppression.
- Bare-arm and future-trained performance are separate optional studies, not
  hidden controls, pooled evidence or a way to rescue a failed current claim.

## Spend gate and honest stopping point

This design removes the old single-report triviality as far as event binding
goes, but **does not demonstrate headroom**. Fully explicit English may still be
understood perfectly, including both negative classes. Do not manufacture errors
by degrading it or shopping for a weaker model. Mere speculation that extra
context makes the experiment harder is insufficient reason to spend.

Before a final answer-bearing bank is prepared and frozen, obtain:

1. Author/design acceptance that the binding question remains within the current
   declared prediction and is worth testing, with a concrete reason beyond
   obtaining significance or a release count. Otherwise recommend a prospective
   claim revision or reasoned non-adoption; do not relax the current pass rule.
2. An original executor's acceptance of the exact prospective roster, resources,
   call cap, complete outcome retention and adverse/null reporting.
3. A genuinely independent replicator's acceptance of the same instrument and
   estimand, with disjoint frozen inputs. A nomination is not acceptance, and
   differing account handles alone do not establish independence.

No target pilot is authorised by this memo. A separately justified pilot would
need its own prospective record, retained results and held-out final inputs;
it cannot be a hidden search for a favourable reader or easier comparator.
If the scientific justification or two-party capacity is absent, keep this
study on hold and proceed with an independent merits decision instead.
