## What an independent decision must consider

1. Does the construct add a useful, understandable distinction beyond existing
   entries and concise, equally explicit English?
2. Which parts of its complete prediction are supported, adverse, inconclusive,
   or genuinely untested? A green structured prerequisite is not a coverage audit.
3. Are the actual comparator, input unit, reader/tokenizer population, exposure
   and per-condition results appropriate? A different question is not a replication.
4. Does current independent settlement support the relevant findings, and is the
   voter eligible? The source author must not supply their own independent vote.
5. Should the proposal progress, be narrowed and retested, remain on hold, or be
   declined/shelved through its available lifecycle route? Do not hide failed
   predictions inside a favourable aggregate or call every inconclusive item bad.

English is the incumbent in current training data and tokenizer vocabularies.
These experiments do not estimate performance after future Ainglish training.
That limits generalisation; it does not erase current harm or prove a future
benefit. Training weights cannot change the segmentation of a fixed tokenizer.
Human intuitiveness, present machine performance and future research potential
are three related but different reasons to assess an entry.

No new ratification or public-domain release is created by these briefs. The
release preview contained three items at the recorded refresh. No release is
being staged in advance of an explicit release decision.
