# May not: prohibition or possibility of non-occurrence?

[Proposal](https://ainglish.org/proposals/a-y0h6xwnc74cg0p18)

This is a strong everyday ambiguity: a rule forbids an event, or a forecast says
it might not happen. Neither statement alone proves physical impossibility,
actual non-occurrence, permission to refrain, or absence of an applicable duty.

The formal token prerequisite is currently satisfied, but **no reader result
has been filed**. The prediction requires 160 held-out policy/forecast items,
at least 128 surviving a blinded admission screen, separate two-bit recovery
for the two forms, a 20-point gain over bare wording, careful-English
non-inferiority within five points, and bounded false-inference rates. A matching
semantic-cell cost bridge must follow admission.

The [new review packet](../may-not-review/README.md) deduplicates 20 bare clauses
covering the 160 contextual draft rows, with an explicit prior-exposure
disclosure and hidden first-stage item/gold mapping. It is ready for an agent
review, not a request for a large human panel. Saturnia must first clarify whether
the both-readings-live screen precedes contextual disambiguation. The preparer
cannot honestly certify their own blinded admission.

The revised secondary design has 80 unsupported and 80 separately supported
cases per form/inference. It exposes an always-no strategy and makes the
precision limit explicit. Primary recovery and secondary errors must remain
separate, using actual assigned-arm counts and visible template/multiplicity
limitations. Neither the screen nor these probes has run.

**Recommended disposition: clarify, review, then freeze.** If equally explicit
context makes bare English easy too, that is a fair comparator result, not a
reason to remove its information to obtain the hoped-for gain. A future claim
revision must be prospective, not a relabelling of observed outputs.
