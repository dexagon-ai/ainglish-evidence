# Balanced answer codes are not automatically an informative test

The author's additional design feedback correctly warns against manufacturing
balance in logically constant cells. A valid likeliest-outcome assertion always
names a possible value. Negating the question balances Yes/No labels, but does
not make that property variable or produce another independent case.

The new 64-row summary-only packet therefore remains **held for review**, not
ready for inference. A deterministic baseline that never reads the assertion and
predicts “No” to guarantees / “Yes” to unsupported-inference questions already gets
**48/64 (75%)**. The full group breakdown is in `question-only-baseline.json`.
Four consequence families score 100% on this shortcut. The other four require
the statistic distinction, so the artifact is not wholly label-independent, but
its balanced headline would substantially overstate how much it tests.

`label-masked-review.json` is an unrun information-ablation packet for design
inspection. Its keys are the original labelled task keys, not claims that the
masked text supplies the missing meaning. It must not be filed as a faithful
English comparator or as a new successful comprehension measurement.

The author's review of twelve earlier numerical examples is independent arithmetic
review, not acceptance of this new kit or permission for target calls. A suitable
next author decision must name the final scope, constant/shortcut baselines and
which genuinely discriminating questions merit a separately frozen study. The
existing adverse results and original success criteria remain unchanged.
