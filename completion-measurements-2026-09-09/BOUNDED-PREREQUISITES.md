# Bounded prerequisites: prospective shadow specification

This is a report-only research design. It does not change any current proposal,
settlement rule, allowance or interpretation of an adverse measurement.

## Two different questions

“Does another sample reproduce this estimate?” and “Is the declared language cost
within an allowance?” are not interchangeable. The current legacy point rule allows
fresh samples and different manifests; it does **not** require identical digits.
Conversely, two estimates below an allowance do not establish that the population
mean is below it. An allowance here is Ainglish-minus-English tokens per complete
message, not GPU time, download bandwidth or experiment expenditure.

## Minimum prospective contract

Name the proposal version, each form, each tokenizer, the complete meaning-preserving
English renderer, target population, independent sampling unit and any clustering.
State the allowance, sampling frame, missing/invalid-input rule, sample size and
one-time stopping rule before collecting the sample. Retain every encoding and pole,
including null/adverse results. No post-result comparator padding, changed roster,
favourable-only sample selection or retrospective confidence interval is acceptable.

A possible conservative pilot, **only when its assumptions can be defended**, uses
independent bounded cluster means. If each cluster mean lies in the prospectively
fixed interval [a,b], use U = mean + (b-a) sqrt(log(K/alpha)/(2n)), where n counts
independent clusters and K counts every cell/replication conclusion covered by the
family. Require each U to meet that cell's allowance. This is a one-sided
Hoeffding bound with union-bound allocation, not a standard error inferred from
the spread of a handful of tokenizers. [Hoeffding's original paper](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf).

The assumptions matter more than the formula. Surface variants of one scenario
cannot be declared independent just because their IDs differ. A declared
`member_span` is not a sampling interval. Ordinary clustered-data inference also
warns that within-cluster dependence can make naive precision badly misleading;
few clusters remain difficult. [Cameron and Miller](https://cameron.econ.ucdavis.edu/research/Cameron_Miller_JHR_2015_February.pdf).

For natural language with an undefended population or support bound, report
**not assessable under this rule**, not a numeric assurance. A finite closed frame
and exhaustive count is a distinct design; neither proves future natural usage.
Choosing support after seeing costs would invalidate the guarantee. The formula
may be far too conservative to be useful with small, genuinely independent samples.

## Falsifiers and decisions

- Wrong comparator, changed estimand, missing prerequisite, incomplete named cells
  or unrecoverable provenance: inadmissible for this pilot, not a missing favourable result.
- A simultaneous upper bound above the allowance: not established, even if its
  point estimate is below. A true-cost excess in known-truth simulation is a false
  pass whenever the candidate rule accepts it.
- Cloning records raises apparent confidence: the method is handling dependence
  incorrectly. Clustering must be fixed before outcomes, not selected to obtain a pass.
- Satisfying this cost rule still supplies no comprehension, learnability, adoption,
  tokenizer-training or future-model-performance evidence.

The frozen CPU simulation tests six deliberately different rules on known-truth
populations, including rare tails, one failing form/tokenizer cell, few independent
clusters, and cloned renderings. It reports all conditions and Monte Carlo uncertainty.
Point agreement is an abstract diagnostic here, not a full reproduction of register
admission, independence, stratified settlement or governance. No simulated receipt
will be submitted as a language measurement. Results will inform whether a new
prospective protocol discussion is worthwhile; they cannot backdate a new gate.
