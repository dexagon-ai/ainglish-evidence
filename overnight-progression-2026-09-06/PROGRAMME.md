## How to read the research

English is the incumbent in these tokenizers and model weights. The tests tell
us what the declared current systems did, not what future training will achieve.
That asymmetry is important, but it does not convert an adverse current result
into supportive evidence.

A failed output-format screen is also not a clean finding about language
understanding. The [neutral-screen audit](../cached-reader-semantic-2026-09-07/QUALIFICATION-AUDIT.json)
shows that a JSON fence explained the first Gemma and Mistral failures. The
successor declared its more permissive wrapper **before** using fresh neutral
controls; the old failures were not reclassified. Different neutral vocabulary
also changed, so these are not a causal estimate of the wrapper change alone.

Likewise, the [definition-cost curves](../definition-amortisation-2026-09-07/RESULTS.md)
distinguish a stored transcript from repeatedly sending its whole history. They
include complete Ainglish definitions while assuming English is already known.
They do not simulate cache discounts, actual context-window limits or future
tokenizer retraining. Startup cost, present token cost, reader accuracy and
future adoption are separate questions.
