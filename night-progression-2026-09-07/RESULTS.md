# Results, with the distinctions retained

All reader comparisons were frozen together before the first target call. Two
exact qualified cached reader interfaces were used; four calibration gates passed.
No download, substitution, tuned retry or selective omission was used.

| Frozen comparison | Ainglish minus English, pp | Official interval | Scope |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Price/allocation vs careful English | -15.8175 | [-23.6962, -7.6388] | Joint recovery; positive forms and ordinary-negation extensions separated below |
| Proposal/decision vs careful English | -42.1650 | [-52.1172, -31.8140] | Joint historical-status/choice/force question |
| Verdict tags vs careful English | -15.6350 | [-25.8929, -5.0318] | Same visible result anchor in both arms |
| Verdict tags vs anchored bare `failed` | +14.9450 | [+4.7216, +25.1472] | Not an independent replication of the careful-English comparison |

Absolute accuracy matters. English/Ainglish accuracy was 48.68%/32.87% in the
price/allocation study and 57.30%/15.13% in the proposal/decision study. These were
challenging joint questions, not simple human-recognition polls. Price/allocation
per-stratum deltas were -16.52 (`no-charge`, positive), -1.41 (negated), -2.22
(`available-now`, positive), and -43.12 (negated). The negation extension is not
the original proposal's entire positive-form claim.

For verdict tags, English/Ainglish accuracy was 79.09%/63.46% against careful
English and 50.04%/64.98% against bare wording with an explicit anchor. Against
bare wording, `no-verdict` improved by 37.92 points while `verdict-fail` lost 8.03;
neither the aggregate gain nor that contrast establishes per-tag success against
careful English. The exact per-form results and cell receipts remain available.

These cold-reading results describe the named present-day quantized models and
authored items. English has incumbent training/tokenizer exposure; this is not a
prediction of future Ainglish-trained performance, nor proof that training will
erase these losses. The results do not alone establish human intuitiveness or
justify rejecting a construct across all future uses. They do show specific
current claims that need independent replication, revision or narrower support.
